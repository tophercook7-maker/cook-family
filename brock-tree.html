Deeper still — the Swiss root

Above Rudolph the line runs back through the same family, in Bözen & the Bözberg parish, canton Aargau — the Swiss church books write the name Brack, the old spelling of Brock: Caspar Brock (b. 1646) → his father Caspar Brock “Untervogt” (b. 1613 — an Untervogt was the village's deputy magistrate) → Heinrich Brock “von Bözberg” (b. ~1578), reaching into the mid-1500s.

These come from a compiled “Brack in Bözen” family genealogy held on FamilySearch — a real Swiss source, but hand-copied notes rather than the original parish registers, and with some tangled dates. The well-supported frontier of this line — not yet independently proven.

The original Bözen church books do survive and cover exactly these lives — Register I, 1562–1604, and Register II, 1604–1685 (baptisms & marriages for Bözen, Effingen, Elfingen and neighbors) — but they are kept in the Bözen municipal archive (microfilm at the Aargau State Archive, Aarau) and are not online. Confirming Heinrich, Caspar and Caspar in them means a page-by-page read of those films in Switzerland.

The Journey

Bözen, Switzerland (1600s) → sailed to Pennsylvania on the ship Hope, 1733 → Virginia → North Carolina → Georgia, the Habersham & Gilmer hill country → Arkansas, Polk County (from the mid-1800s to today).

Nine proven generations, and a documented Swiss family reaching back toward the mid-1500s beyond that. The Brock side reaches far further than the Cook side — across an ocean, to a mountain parish in Switzerland.
